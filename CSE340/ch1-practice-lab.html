01Core concepts, illustrated

The five ideas that carry almost every calculation mark.

Performance = 1 / time

"X is n times faster than Y" means n = TimeY / TimeX. A: 10 s, B: 15 s → A is 1.5× faster. Always divide, never subtract.

The CPU time equation

CPU time = IC × CPI / clock rate. IC comes from the program, compiler and ISA. CPI from the hardware and instruction mix. Clock rate from the hardware and technology.

Dynamic power = C × V² × f

Voltage is squared, so dropping voltage was the main power saver, until leakage below about 1 V created the power wall around 2004.

Amdahl's law

Tnew = Taffected/n + Tunaffected. The unaffected part sets a floor; max speedup = Told / Tunaffected.

SPEC uses the geometric mean

SPECratio = reference ÷ execution time. The geometric mean of ratios gives the same verdict whichever machine is the reference.

Cost per die

Wafer cost ÷ (dies × yield), with yield = 1/(1 + D×A)N. Bigger dies mean fewer dies and lower yield, so cost rises faster than area.

05Memory hooks

Seven great ideas: "A C P P P H D"

Abstraction, Common case fast, Parallelism, Pipelining, Prediction, Hierarchy of memories, Dependability via redundancy. "A Common Processor Pipelines, Predicts, Hides Delays."

Five components: "I O M D C"

Input, Output, Memory, Datapath, Control. "I Only Make Dumb Code." Datapath + Control = processor.

Power wall in 4 beats

Faster clock → more power → lower the voltage → voltage hits ~1 V, leakage wins → multicore.

Amdahl = a traffic jam

A faster motorway does not help if half your trip is the same red light. The red light is Tunaffected.

Yield exponent

"Big die, big risk": every extra cm² is another chance to land on a defect, and N makes it worse.

MIPS lies

"Meaningless Indicator of Processor Speed": instruction counts differ across ISAs, so compare seconds.
